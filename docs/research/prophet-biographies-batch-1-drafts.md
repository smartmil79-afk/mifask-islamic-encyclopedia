# Prophet Biographies — Batch 1 Editorial Drafts

**Status:** Draft for Qur'anic reference checking and scholarly editorial review  
**Scope:** Adam, Nuh, Ibrahim, Yusuf, Musa, and Yunus (peace be upon them all)  
**Method:** Original summaries based primarily on the Qur'anic passages listed with each section. These drafts deliberately avoid adding popular narrative details that are not established by the cited passages. They are not marked verified or ready for publication.

## Editorial rules for this batch

- The Qur'an is the primary narrative source for these drafts.
- Verse references are attached to the claims they support; editors must compare every reference against MIFASK's stored Arabic text and chosen English translation before publication.
- No hadith reports have been added in this batch. Any future supplementary report must identify its collection and exact location, and include a reliability note where appropriate.
- Interpretive points are phrased cautiously. Disputed identifications and details are not asserted as fact.
- Lessons are editorial reflections drawn from the cited narrative, not quotations from revelation.
- The article audio script must be generated only from the final approved article text.

## 1. Adam عليه السلام

**Suggested title:** Prophet Adam عليه السلام  
**Suggested summary:** The Qur'an describes Adam's creation, the honour Allah granted him, the test in the Garden, and his repentance. His account introduces human responsibility, humility, and the mercy of Allah.

### Biography draft

The Qur'an presents Adam as the first human being in the account of humanity's beginning. Allah informed the angels that He would place a succeeding authority on earth. Allah taught Adam the names, demonstrating knowledge granted by his Creator. The angels were commanded to prostrate in honour of the command, and they did so except Iblis, who refused out of arrogance. The Qur'an describes Iblis as being among the jinn and as disobedient to his Lord. (Qur'an 2:30–34; 7:11–18; 15:26–44; 18:50)

Adam and his spouse were given a place in the Garden and permitted to enjoy its provisions, while being instructed not to approach a particular tree. Iblis tempted them, and they ate from it. The Qur'an describes their awareness of their vulnerability and their turning back to Allah after the mistake. Their supplication acknowledges that they had wronged themselves and that without Allah's forgiveness and mercy they would be among the losers. (Qur'an 2:35–36; 7:19–23; 20:115–121)

Allah accepted Adam's repentance and then sent them down to earthly life, where guidance would come and people would be accountable for how they responded to it. The Qur'an therefore connects the beginning of human life on earth with divine guidance, moral responsibility, and the possibility of repentance. (Qur'an 2:37–39; 20:122–123)

Adam's account should not be expanded with details that the cited Qur'anic passages do not establish. The Qur'an does not give his spouse a personal name in these passages, nor does it provide a complete chronology of their earthly lives. Such details should not be presented as Qur'anic facts without a separately identified and evaluated source.

### Lessons and reflections

- Knowledge is a gift from Allah and should lead to humility, not arrogance. (Qur'an 2:31–33)
- Arrogance can lead a person to reject a clear command. (Qur'an 2:34; 7:12–18)
- Human beings are responsible for their choices, but repentance remains open to those who turn sincerely to Allah. (Qur'an 7:23; 20:122)
- Guidance from Allah is central to human life. (Qur'an 2:38; 20:123)

### Reference map

- Creation, earthly responsibility, and teaching Adam: Qur'an 2:30–33.
- The command to the angels and Iblis's refusal: Qur'an 2:34; 7:11–18; 15:28–44.
- The Garden, the tree, and the temptation: Qur'an 2:35–36; 7:19–22; 20:115–121.
- Repentance and descent: Qur'an 2:37–39; 7:23–25; 20:122–123.

**Editorial caution:** Keep the distinction between the Qur'anic account and later reports clear. Do not add unsupported names, locations, dates, or descriptions.

## 2. Nuh عليه السلام

**Suggested title:** Prophet Nuh عليه السلام  
**Suggested summary:** Nuh called his people to worship Allah alone over a long period, warned them, and remained steadfast. The Qur'an recounts the Ark, the flood, and the rescue of those who believed.

### Biography draft

Allah sent Nuh to his people with a clear message: worship Allah, be mindful of Him, and obey His messenger. Nuh warned them of accountability and called them to seek Allah's forgiveness. He described calling them openly and privately, by night and by day, showing the persistence of his mission. The Qur'an states that he remained among his people for a thousand years less fifty before the flood overtook them. (Qur'an 7:59–64; 10:71–73; 11:25–26; 23:23–30; 29:14–15; 71:1–20)

The people who rejected Nuh's message opposed him and those who believed with him. The Qur'an records Nuh's patience and his appeal to Allah after his people continued in rejection. Allah instructed him to construct the Ark under divine guidance. When the command came, Nuh and the believers boarded, and the flood came as Allah had decreed. (Qur'an 11:36–44; 23:27–29; 26:105–122; 54:9–15)

The account also describes Nuh's son, who did not join the believers on the Ark. Nuh called to him, but the son refused and was overwhelmed by the flood. Allah explained to Nuh that the son was not among the family promised deliverance in the sense Nuh had understood, and Nuh turned to Allah in humility. This passage teaches that faith and obedience, not family connection alone, determine a person's response to Allah's message. (Qur'an 11:42–47)

Allah rescued Nuh and those with him and made the event a sign for later generations. The Qur'an praises Nuh as a grateful servant and records that peace was upon him among the worlds. (Qur'an 10:73; 17:3; 37:75–82)

### Lessons and reflections

- Calling to Allah requires patience, clarity, and persistence. (Qur'an 71:5–9)
- A person's response to truth is their own responsibility; family relationship does not replace faith. (Qur'an 11:42–47)
- Nuh's story presents gratitude and reliance upon Allah as qualities of a believer. (Qur'an 17:3; 37:80)
- The account warns against arrogance and persistent rejection after a warning has been made clear. (Qur'an 7:64; 11:32–39)

### Reference map

- Nuh's message and the response of his people: Qur'an 7:59–64; 11:25–36; 23:23–25; 71:1–20.
- Construction of the Ark and the flood: Qur'an 11:36–44; 23:27–29; 26:119–120; 54:9–15.
- Nuh's son and the lesson about faith: Qur'an 11:42–47.
- Rescue and remembrance of Nuh: Qur'an 10:73; 17:3; 37:75–82.

**Editorial caution:** Do not add the Ark's dimensions, landing location, the names of Nuh's family members beyond those named in the Qur'an, or other details unless supported by separately assessed evidence.

## 3. Ibrahim عليه السلام

**Suggested title:** Prophet Ibrahim عليه السلام  
**Suggested summary:** Ibrahim called his people away from idol worship, demonstrated trust in Allah, and became an example of sincere devotion. The Qur'an connects his story with the Ka'bah, prayer, hospitality, and his family's role in worship.

### Biography draft

The Qur'an describes Ibrahim as a truthful prophet who questioned the worship of idols and called his people to worship Allah alone. He reasoned with his father and his community, asking how they could worship things that could neither hear nor benefit them. In another passage, he reflected on the created heavenly bodies and declared that he turned himself toward the One who created the heavens and the earth. The Qur'anic account presents this reasoning as a rejection of associating partners with Allah. (Qur'an 6:74–83; 19:41–50; 21:51–67; 26:69–82)

Ibrahim's people opposed his message. The Qur'an recounts their attempt to punish him by fire, but Allah made the fire coolness and safety for him. The narrative emphasizes that the outcome was by Allah's command and that the plots of those who opposed him did not overcome Allah's will. (Qur'an 21:68–70; 29:16–27; 37:83–98)

Ibrahim continued his devotion to Allah and was tested through commands that demonstrated his submission. The Qur'an records his migration for the sake of his Lord and his prayers for a righteous family and a secure place of worship. With Ismail, he raised the foundations of the House and prayed that their work be accepted. The Qur'an also records Ibrahim's prayer for a community devoted to Allah and for a messenger to be sent among his descendants. (Qur'an 2:124–129; 14:35–41; 22:26–29)

The Qur'an recounts a profound test involving Ibrahim and his son. Ibrahim told his son about the vision, and the son responded with willingness to submit to Allah's command. When they had both submitted, Allah called out and ransomed the son with a great sacrifice. The passage does not name the son in the account itself; therefore this article does not present a disputed identification as though it were explicit Qur'anic wording. (Qur'an 37:100–111)

Ibrahim is praised as a devoted leader and as one who fulfilled his responsibilities. The Qur'an describes him as neither belonging to the later religious communities by their historical labels, but as inclining wholly to truth and submitting to Allah. His example is connected to sincere worship, hospitality, prayer, and trust in Allah. (Qur'an 3:65–68; 16:120–123; 60:4–6)

### Lessons and reflections

- Worship should be directed to the Creator, not to created things. (Qur'an 6:74–79)
- Faith can require courage when a community resists the truth. (Qur'an 21:68–70)
- Supplication for one's family and future generations is part of Ibrahim's example. (Qur'an 14:35–41)
- Worship and service should be undertaken sincerely, while asking Allah to accept one's efforts. (Qur'an 2:127)
- Submission to Allah is the central theme of Ibrahim's tests. (Qur'an 37:102–107)

### Reference map

- Ibrahim's call and reasoning: Qur'an 6:74–83; 19:41–50; 21:51–67.
- The fire and Allah's protection: Qur'an 21:68–70; 29:24; 37:97–98.
- Migration, family prayers, and the Ka'bah: Qur'an 2:124–129; 14:35–41; 22:26–29.
- The test involving his son: Qur'an 37:100–111.
- Ibrahim's qualities and example: Qur'an 3:65–68; 16:120–123; 60:4–6.

**Editorial caution:** The identity of the son in Qur'an 37:100–111 is discussed in exegetical literature. Do not state one identification as explicit Qur'anic wording. Check any supplementary report and tafsir attribution before including it.

## 4. Yusuf عليه السلام

**Suggested title:** Prophet Yusuf عليه السلام  
**Suggested summary:** Surah Yusuf tells a connected account of Yusuf's early dream, family separation, trials in Egypt, imprisonment, and eventual reunion. His story highlights patience, integrity, forgiveness, and trust in Allah.

### Biography draft

Surah Yusuf begins with Yusuf telling his father about a dream involving eleven stars, the sun, and the moon. His father recognized that the dream carried significance and advised him not to relate it to his brothers. The Qur'an then describes how jealousy led Yusuf's brothers to plot against him and separate him from their father. They returned with a false account, while Yaqub responded with patience and sought Allah's help. (Qur'an 12:4–18)

Yusuf was taken to Egypt and came into the household of a man who instructed his wife to treat him honourably. As Yusuf grew, Allah granted him sound judgment and knowledge. He faced a serious moral test in that household and sought Allah's protection. The Qur'an records his refusal to betray trust and his preference for what was right, even when the situation brought hardship. (Qur'an 12:19–35)

Yusuf was imprisoned, where he continued to call people to worship Allah alone. When two fellow prisoners asked him to interpret their dreams, he first explained the foundation of his faith and then gave the interpretations. Later, the king's dream led to Yusuf being summoned. Yusuf did not seek immediate release without first making the truth of the earlier accusation clear. The matter was investigated, and the women acknowledged what had happened. (Qur'an 12:36–53)

Yusuf was appointed to a position of responsibility over the storehouses of the land. During the years of hardship, his brothers came to Egypt seeking provisions. After a series of events, Yusuf revealed his identity to them. He forgave them and did not reproach them for what they had done. He sent his shirt to his father, and the family eventually came together in Egypt. (Qur'an 12:54–100)

The dream Yusuf had as a child was fulfilled, and he recognized Allah's care throughout the events of his life. The Surah closes with reminders that these accounts contain lessons for people who reflect and that the message of the messengers is grounded in truth. (Qur'an 12:100–111)

### Lessons and reflections

- A difficult beginning does not prevent a person from remaining faithful and upright. (Qur'an 12:18; 12:90)
- Yusuf resisted wrongdoing and sought Allah's protection. (Qur'an 12:23–24)
- Knowledge and ability should be accompanied by integrity and responsibility. (Qur'an 12:54–57)
- Yusuf's forgiveness of his brothers is a powerful example of restraint and mercy. (Qur'an 12:92)
- The Surah repeatedly connects patience with trust in Allah. (Qur'an 12:18; 12:83; 12:90)

### Reference map

- The dream and the brothers' plot: Qur'an 12:4–18.
- Yusuf in Egypt and the moral test: Qur'an 12:19–35.
- Prison, the call to worship Allah, and dream interpretation: Qur'an 12:36–42.
- The king's dream, investigation, and Yusuf's appointment: Qur'an 12:43–57.
- Reunion with his brothers and family: Qur'an 12:58–100.
- Reflections on the narrative: Qur'an 12:101–111.

**Editorial caution:** The Qur'an's Surah Yusuf is the continuous primary narrative. Avoid inserting names, dialogue, dates, or dramatic details from popular retellings unless they are separately sourced and clearly distinguished.

## 5. Musa عليه السلام

**Suggested title:** Prophet Musa عليه السلام  
**Suggested summary:** The Qur'an recounts Musa's rescue as an infant, his life in Madyan, his call to Pharaoh, the deliverance of the Children of Israel, and the guidance given to his people.

### Biography draft

The Qur'an describes how Pharaoh's rule threatened the Children of Israel. Allah inspired Musa's mother to place him in the river when she feared for him, assuring her that he would be returned to her and made a messenger. Musa was found by members of Pharaoh's household, and Allah caused him to be protected. His own mother was brought back to nurse him, fulfilling Allah's promise. (Qur'an 20:38–40; 28:3–13)

As a young man, Musa left Egypt after an incident in which a man died during a struggle. He sought Allah's forgiveness and later travelled to Madyan. There he helped two women water their flock, was invited to their family, and eventually entered into an agreement to work for a period of time. The Qur'an records these events without giving every detail of his years in Madyan. (Qur'an 28:14–28)

On his return journey, Musa saw a fire and approached it. Allah called him and appointed him as a messenger, commanding him to worship Allah and establish prayer in His remembrance. Musa was given signs and asked Allah to strengthen him through his brother Harun, who would assist him in the mission. (Qur'an 20:9–36; 27:7–12; 28:29–35)

Musa and Harun went to Pharaoh and called him to release the Children of Israel and worship Allah. Pharaoh rejected the message, and a contest with the magicians followed. When the magicians witnessed the sign, they believed in the Lord of Musa and Harun despite Pharaoh's threats. The Qur'an recounts further signs and the continued resistance of Pharaoh and his people. (Qur'an 7:103–126; 20:42–76; 26:10–51)

Allah commanded Musa to lead the Children of Israel away. When Pharaoh and his forces pursued them, Allah parted the sea for Musa and those with him, then caused the pursuing forces to be overwhelmed. The Qur'an presents this deliverance as a sign of Allah's power and the failure of oppression before His decree. (Qur'an 2:50; 10:90–92; 20:77–79; 26:52–68)

After the deliverance, Musa was given the appointed meeting with his Lord and received guidance. During his absence, a group among his people fell into the worship of the calf. Harun had urged them not to follow that path, and Musa returned deeply concerned. The Qur'an describes Musa's response, his appeal to Allah, and the correction of the community. (Qur'an 7:142–154; 20:83–98)

The Qur'an contains many further passages about Musa's leadership, the obligations placed upon the Children of Israel, and the lessons of gratitude, patience, and obedience. This article should link to those passages rather than compressing every episode into an oversimplified chronology. (Qur'an 2:40–61; 5:20–26; 18:60–82; 28:43–46)

### Lessons and reflections

- Allah's care can reach a person even in circumstances that appear overwhelming. (Qur'an 28:7–13)
- Musa asked Allah for help and clarity before undertaking a difficult mission. (Qur'an 20:25–36)
- Speaking truth to power requires courage and reliance upon Allah. (Qur'an 20:43–46)
- Signs do not benefit those who persist in arrogance and refusal. (Qur'an 7:132–136)
- Leadership includes patience, guidance, and correcting wrongdoing with wisdom. (Qur'an 7:142–154)

### Reference map

- Musa's infancy and return to his mother: Qur'an 20:38–40; 28:3–13.
- His departure to Madyan: Qur'an 28:14–28.
- The call and mission with Harun: Qur'an 20:9–36; 27:7–12; 28:29–35.
- Pharaoh, the signs, and the magicians: Qur'an 7:103–126; 20:42–76; 26:10–51.
- The Exodus and the sea: Qur'an 2:50; 10:90–92; 20:77–79; 26:52–68.
- The calf and the community: Qur'an 7:142–154; 20:83–98.

**Editorial caution:** Musa's story appears across many Surahs and is not presented in one uninterrupted chronological account. Avoid inventing a single detailed timeline where the Qur'an does not supply one. The account in Qur'an 18:60–82 should be treated as a separate episode and carefully explained with its own context.

## 6. Yunus عليه السلام

**Suggested title:** Prophet Yunus عليه السلام  
**Suggested summary:** The Qur'an recounts Yunus's departure, his trial at sea, his supplication, and Allah's mercy. It also highlights the unusual response of his people, who believed and were granted respite.

### Biography draft

The Qur'an identifies Yunus as a messenger and refers to his departure in a state of distress. The account describes him as one who was drawn into the lot and then swallowed by a great fish. The Qur'an does not provide the name of the sea creature or a detailed travel narrative, so this article remains with the wording and events established by the verses. (Qur'an 10:98; 21:87; 37:139–142)

In the darkness of his trial, Yunus called upon Allah, declaring His perfection and acknowledging his own wrongdoing. Allah answered him and delivered him from distress. The Qur'an presents this as a sign of Allah's mercy and states that He saves the believers. (Qur'an 21:87–88; 37:143–144)

Allah caused Yunus to be cast onto the shore while he was ill, and a plant was made to grow over him. He was then sent to a community of many people, who believed; Allah allowed them to enjoy life for a time. This is a distinctive feature of the Qur'anic account, which explicitly mentions the belief of Yunus's people. (Qur'an 37:145–148; 10:98)

The Qur'an also instructs the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ to be patient and not to be like the companion of the fish when he called out in distress. The passage recalls Allah's favour upon Yunus and his being chosen among the righteous. (Qur'an 68:48–50)

### Lessons and reflections

- In distress, a believer turns to Allah and acknowledges His perfection. (Qur'an 21:87)
- Allah's mercy can bring relief after hardship. (Qur'an 21:88; 37:143–148)
- The response of Yunus's people shows that a community's sincere belief can change its outcome. (Qur'an 10:98)
- Patience is an important lesson drawn from the account. (Qur'an 68:48–50)

### Reference map

- Yunus's departure and the trial at sea: Qur'an 21:87; 37:139–142.
- His supplication and deliverance: Qur'an 21:87–88; 37:143–144.
- His recovery and the belief of his people: Qur'an 37:145–148; 10:98.
- The reminder to remain patient: Qur'an 68:48–50.

**Editorial caution:** Do not state the duration of Yunus's time in the fish, the species of the fish, or a detailed location unless a reliable and clearly attributed source is added. The Qur'anic text itself does not provide those details.
